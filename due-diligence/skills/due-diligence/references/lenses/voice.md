# voice — Does it read as a person wrote it, not a model?
> Cites: res_technical-writing.md

## Fires on
General lens (no tag) — select for any prose deliverable, especially AI-generated (Step 0 Q1). This is DD's home turf: AI output is the primary subject, and AI-tells erode a skeptical reader's trust before they judge the substance. Overlaps necessity (both cut filler); voice targets the *machine-generated register* specifically.

## Attacks
- Reflexive hedging: "it's worth noting", "generally speaking", "in many cases", "it's important to remember" used as padding, not because the claim is genuinely uncertain.
- Formulaic triads: everything arriving in threes; parallel list-of-three structures repeated until the rhythm itself signals a template.
- Sycophantic / empty openers: "Great question!", "Absolutely!", throat-clearing that says nothing.
- Empty summarising: "In conclusion, X is a multifaceted/complex topic that requires careful consideration" — a paragraph that restates without adding.
- Inflated register: "delve", "leverage", "robust", "underscore", "navigate the landscape", "in today's fast-paced world" — vocabulary inflation over plain words.
- Uniform rhythm: every paragraph the same length, every sentence the same shape — the tell of unedited generation.

## Measure
AI tells per 1k words, counted against the attack list above (reflexive hedging, formulaic triads,
empty openers, empty summarising, inflated register, uniform rhythm). Report count and rate. Above
2 per 1k = should-fix. Above 5 per 1k, or any in a first paragraph a stakeholder reads = blocker.

## Evidence of attack (clean-pass proof)
Quote the specific tells found (with locations) and the plain-language replacement, or state the prose was checked against the AI-tell list and reads as human-written (name what you looked for — hedging, triads, inflated register, empty summary). "Sounds fine" is not evidence.

## Severity guide
- blocker: rare — only if the AI register is so pervasive the substance is buried or the piece would embarrass in front of the stated reader.
- should-fix: repeated tells (hedging, triads, inflated register, empty conclusion) that a reader would notice and that cheapen the work.
- nit: an isolated "delve"/"leverage" or one hedging phrase.
