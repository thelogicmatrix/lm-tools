# depth-sufficiency — Is anything load-bearing glossed over or under-developed?
> Cites: res_technical-writing.md

## Fires on
General lens (no tag) — select for any substantive artifact making claims or recommendations. The **inverse of necessity**: necessity cuts what's overdeveloped; this flags what's underdeveloped — a consequential point the reader can't act on or verify because the support isn't there. Run them together; they pull in opposite directions toward "as deep as the decision needs."

## Attacks
- Consequential claim asserted in one line: a load-bearing conclusion with no mechanism, evidence, or example behind it — the reader must take it on faith.
- Recommendation with no "how": "we should do X" with no steps, prerequisites, or what it would take — not actionable (pairs with actionability, but here the gap is *depth* not *presence*).
- Unexplained leap: a step in the reasoning that assumes an intermediate the reader isn't given; the argument skips a rung.
- Number with no "why": a figure or result stated without how it was derived or what drives it, where the reader needs that to trust or use it.
- Glossed edge/risk: a known complication, caveat, or failure mode named in passing but not developed enough for the reader to weigh it.
- Section that promises more than it delivers: a heading or lead-in that sets up depth the body never provides.

## Measure
Load-bearing claims or recommendations without support ÷ load-bearing claims. Name what you
counted as load-bearing. Report as "n/N unsupported". Any unsupported claim the reader must act on
= blocker. Any other = should-fix.

## Evidence of attack (clean-pass proof)
Name each load-bearing claim/step/recommendation and confirm it carries enough support to act on or verify — or point to the one-line assertion, missing "how", or skipped reasoning rung. Distinguish from filler: this is information the reader *needs* that's absent, not words that should be cut. State what you judged load-bearing and why.

## Severity guide
- blocker: a load-bearing claim or recommendation is so under-developed the reader can't act on it or would act wrongly; a skipped reasoning step breaks the argument.
- should-fix: a consequential point glossed in one line, a recommendation with no "how", an edge/risk named but not developed.
- nit: a minor point could use one more sentence; a "why" that's inferable but unstated.
