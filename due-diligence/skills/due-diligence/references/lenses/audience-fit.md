# audience-fit — Is it pitched for the stated reader, not the author?
> Cites: res_technical-writing.md

## Fires on
General lens (no tag) — select for any artifact with a real reader whose expertise/role is known from Step 0. Skip only when there's no identifiable audience (pure scratch). An artifact can be clear and complete yet still pitched wrong; this lens checks the *level match*, which clarity (local comprehensibility) does not.

## Attacks
- Over the reader's head: jargon, assumed knowledge, or depth calibrated to the author/an expert when the stated reader is an exec, client, or novice — they can't act on what they can't follow.
- Beneath the reader: over-explaining basics to an expert audience, padding a specialist doc with 101-level background they'll skim past (wastes their time, reads as condescending).
- Wrong register/tone for the medium and relationship: casual where formal is expected (or vice versa), internal shorthand in an external-facing piece.
- Reading level mismatched to audience: dense multi-clause sentences for a public/general reader, or a Flesch-Kincaid grade well above the audience (spot-check for external/public docs).
- Answers a different question than the reader has: framed around what the author finds interesting, not the decision the reader must make.

## Evidence of attack (clean-pass proof)
Name the stated audience (from Step 0) and their expertise level. Point to specific terms/passages that are over- or under-pitched for that reader, or confirm the level matches with examples. For external/public docs, state a spot-checked reading level. "Reads well" without naming the audience is not evidence.

## Severity guide
- blocker: the stated reader genuinely cannot follow or use it (jargon/assumed knowledge they lack), or it answers a question they didn't ask.
- should-fix: register/tone mismatch, or depth noticeably off (over- or under-explained) without blocking use.
- nit: a few terms could be pitched better; tone slightly off in places.
