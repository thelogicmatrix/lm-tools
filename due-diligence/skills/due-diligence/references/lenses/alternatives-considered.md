# alternatives-considered — Were real options weighed, or was one choice rationalized?
> Cites: res_decision-quality.md

## Fires on
Tags: proposal, plan, recommendation. Any artifact that lands on a single recommended path.

## Attacks
- No real alternatives: exactly one option is presented, with competitors (if mentioned at all) dismissed in a line or two rather than scored against the same criteria.
- Cheaper/obvious option skipped: a materially different, lower-cost, or status-quo/do-nothing option was never evaluated as a baseline alternative.
- Absent decision matrix: multiple options exist and warrant comparison, but no weighted/Pugh-style scoring against explicit criteria is shown.
- Post-hoc criteria: comparison criteria appear invented to justify the option already chosen rather than set independently beforehand.
- Criteria/weights set after the winner was known: decision criteria or their weights were fixed (or silently adjusted) once the preferred option was already picked.
- Time-pressure substitution: a time constraint or a single strong advocate substitutes for real comparison — "we didn't have time to consider other options" as the explicit or implicit rationale.
- No "what would have to be true" framing: the recommendation is argued as simply "right" rather than decomposed into the discrete conditions that would have to hold, with evidence that would confirm or kill each.

## Evidence of attack (clean-pass proof)
Name every alternative actually weighed (including status quo/do-nothing), the criteria used, and whether those criteria were stated before or after the winner was evident. State explicitly why the chosen option won on those criteria versus the runners-up — a scoring table or equivalent side-by-side, not prose asserting superiority. If only one option is presented, name the most obvious alternative that's missing and whether it's dismissed with reasoning or just absent. Note whether the recommendation is ever framed as "what would have to be true" conditions with a way to test them, or only as a conclusion to accept.

## Severity guide
- blocker: an obviously-better or materially cheaper option was never considered at all, or the sole option is presented with no comparison whatsoever.
- should-fix: alternatives are named but the comparison is thin — no explicit criteria, no scoring, or criteria that look reverse-engineered to fit the chosen option.
- nit: comparison exists and is substantively fair but the write-up/format (e.g. no table, criteria buried in prose) could be clearer.
